Dashboard Blueprint & Project Details

This project blueprint represents a professional "Customer Billing & Recovery Analytics Dashboard" designed for an Assistant Manager (Billing & Recovery) role in a utility, gas distribution, telecom, or finance company.

1. Executive Summary KPIs

  • Total Customers: Total active accounts billed during the selected period.
  • Total Billed Amount: Total invoice value generated.
  • Total Collected Amount: Total cash/payments received.
  • Outstanding Amount: Total unpaid balances (Billed - Collected).
  • Collection Efficiency (%): Total Collected Amount / Total Billed Amount.
  • Recovery Rate (%): Collections made against past-due arrears / Total past-due arrears.

2. Billing Analysis

  • Monthly Billing Trend: Visualizing revenue generation month-over-month.
  • Revenue by Customer Category: Breakdown by segment (e.g., Residential, Commercial, Industrial).
  • Revenue by Region: Geographical distribution of billing.
  • Top 10 Customers by Billing Amount: Identifying key accounts contributing the most to revenue.

3. Recovery & Collection Analysis

  • Monthly Collections Trend: Tracking cash inflow over time.
  • Collection Efficiency Trend: Month-over-month performance of collections against current billing.
  • Outstanding Balance Trend: Monitoring whether overall debt is growing or shrinking.
  • Recovery Performance by Region: Comparing which regional teams are most effective at recovering dues.

4. Aging Analysis

Aging buckets to manage cash flow and identify bad debt:

  • 0-30 Days (Current): Standard credit period.
  • 31-60 Days (Overdue): First follow-up stage.
  • 61-90 Days (Delinquent): Escalation stage.
  • 90+ Days (High Risk/NPA): Potential bad debt requiring legal/recovery action.

Metrics Shown: Outstanding Amount by Aging Bucket, Number of Customers in Each Bucket, and a Recovery Priority Matrix (mapping outstanding amount vs. days overdue).

5. Customer Analysis

  • Paid vs Unpaid Customers: Ratio of fully paid accounts vs. accounts with balances.
  • High-Risk Customers: Accounts in the 90+ days bucket with balances exceeding a specific threshold.
  • Top Defaulters: The highest value unpaid accounts.
  • Payment Behavior Segmentation: Grouping customers into Prompt Payers, Late Payers, and Chronic Defaulters.

6. Visualizations (Power BI Recommendations)

  • Cards: Use for Executive Summary KPIs (Total Billed, Total Collected, Outstanding, Efficiency %).
  • Line Charts: Use for Monthly Billing Trend, Collections Trend, and Efficiency Trend to show time-series data.
  • Bar/Column Charts: Use for Revenue by Region, Recovery Performance by Region, and Outstanding Amount by Aging Bucket.
  • Donut/Pie Charts: Use for Revenue by Customer Category and Paid vs Unpaid Customers.
  • Matrix/Tables: Use for Top 10 Customers, Top Defaulters, and High-Risk Customers.
  • Scatter Plots/Heat Maps: Use for the Recovery Priority Matrix (X-axis: Days Overdue, Y-axis: Outstanding Amount, Bubble Size: Number of Invoices).

7. DAX Measures

Foundational DAX formulas for the dashboard:

Total Billing = SUM(Billing[BilledAmount])
Total Collections = SUM(Collections[CollectedAmount])
Outstanding Amount = [Total Billing] - [Total Collections]
Collection Efficiency % = DIVIDE([Total Collections], [Total Billing], 0)
Recovery Rate % = DIVIDE(SUM(Collections[ArrearsCollected]), SUM(Billing[PreviousArrears]), 0)
Average Days Outstanding = AVERAGEX(Customers, Customers[DaysOutstanding])

(Alternatively, standard DSO: (Outstanding Amount / Total Billed) * Number of Days in Period)

9. Interview Preparation (Q&A)

Q1. What was the primary objective of this dashboard?

To provide visibility into the billing lifecycle, track collection efficiency, identify bottlenecks in cash flow, and highlight high-risk, aging accounts so the recovery team can prioritize their efforts effectively.

Q2. How do you differentiate between Collection Efficiency and Recovery Rate?

Collection Efficiency measures how much of the current month's billing was collected. Recovery Rate measures how much of the historical overdue/arrears was successfully recovered in the current period.

Q3. Why is Aging Analysis important in a utility/telecom business?

It categorizes outstanding debt by time elapsed. The older a debt gets (e.g., 90+ days), the harder it is to collect and the higher the risk of it becoming bad debt. It dictates where the recovery team should focus legal or disconnection efforts.

Q4. How did you handle incomplete or messy data from Excel?

I used Power Query to clean the data—removing duplicates, handling null values in billing dates, standardizing customer categories, and ensuring date formats were correct before loading the data model into Power BI.

Q5. Can you explain the DAX function DIVIDE and why you used it over the standard / operator?

I used DIVIDE(Numerator, Denominator, 0) because it handles "divide by zero" errors gracefully. If there is no billing data for a specific slice, it returns 0 or blank instead of throwing an Infinity error, which would break the visual.

Q6. What visual would you use to show the trend of Collection Efficiency over the last 12 months, and why?

A Line Chart. Line charts are the best practice for displaying continuous time-series data, allowing stakeholders to quickly spot seasonal trends or sudden drops in efficiency.

Q7. If you noticed a sudden spike in 90+ days outstanding for a specific region, what would your next step be?

I would use the dashboard to drill down into that specific region to see if the spike is driven by a few large commercial/industrial customers or a widespread issue among residential customers, and immediately alert the regional recovery manager.

Q8. What makes a dashboard "Professional" for an Assistant Manager?

A professional dashboard follows a top-down approach: high-level KPIs at the top, trend analysis in the middle, and actionable granular detail (like Top Defaulters tables) at the bottom. It also uses a clean color palette, consistent formatting, and avoids chart clutter.

Q9. How do you define a "High-Risk Customer"?

In this model, a high-risk customer is defined by two criteria: they have balances sitting in the 61-90 or 90+ days aging buckets, and their total outstanding amount exceeds a specific threshold (e.g., top 10% of outstanding value).

Q10. How does this dashboard help the company's bottom line?

By shifting the recovery team's focus from reactive to proactive. By clearly identifying top defaulters and aging buckets, the team can target high-value accounts sooner, accelerating cash inflow and reducing bad debt write-offs.
